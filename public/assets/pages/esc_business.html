Why are they necessary for an architecture practice?

Architecture, as defined by the Webster New International Dictionary (2nd Edition, 1950), encompasses the [a]rt or science of building; A method or style of building; Construction [of a] ... frame or structure; Architectural product. Architecture requires the creation of a product or structure; for the Enterprise Architect, the product is collaboration with the stakeholders.

Stakeholders play a leadership role in driving the business towards its mission. They are involved in all aspects of the business, including defining processes, technology, and the people responsible for executing these processes. The following table identifies key stakeholder groups and their roles within the enterprise architecture practice.

Key Stakeholder Groups in Enterprise Architecture
C-Suite Officers One group of stakeholders is the C-Suite officers. Each department (line, business unit, etc.) has a specific role in the enterprise, and all are dependent on IT in some capacity. The various C-level officers are accountable to the CEO for providing business services which taken together ARE the enterprise. They are entrusted to manage efficiently, ensuring cost-effectiveness and maximum productivity.
Business Unit Heads The Business unit heads are a particularly import stakeholders for the Enterprise Architect. Business Architecture is a partner of Enterprise Architecture. This partnership defines the business functions needed to support the business processes. The Business Architecture/Enterprise Architecture team works closely with Business Unit Heads to understand their needs and ensure that the business functions align with the overall business strategy.
Subject Matter Experts This group goes by different names across organziations but the role remains the same: they are the experts on both how the business processes are supported and on the user iterfaces of the application services and structures of the data objects needed to provide the business function. They are invaulable to the architecture practice in understanding what that the Application Service provides, and often how the sytesms are used. They are quite knowledgeable about baseline and also aware of user pain points.
Technology Engineers Implement and maintain technology solutions that support business processes and objectives.
Users Utilize the products and services provided by the organization. Provide feedback and requirements to guide business and technology decisions.
