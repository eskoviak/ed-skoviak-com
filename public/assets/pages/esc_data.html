Data architecture has become a critical pivot point for the success of the enterprise architecture (EA) practice as organizations strive to regain strategic relevance. Modern EA must shift its identity from producing static drawings and documentation to driving decisions, investments, and organizational outcomes. However, traditional data platform architectures—specifically centralized, monolithic data lakes—have become a major bottleneck, often failing to scale and creating measurable business impact: data science teams wait 6-9 months for data access, analytics initiatives miss market windows, and business units resort to shadow IT solutions that create compliance risks. Consequently, for an EA practice to successfully influence portfolios and risk mitigation, it must move beyond viewing data platforms as mere repositories and instead treat data as a dynamic, strategic asset integrated into the business's decision-making process.

To address the failure modes of centralized data lakes, organizations are increasingly adopting a data mesh architecture, which represents a paradigm shift toward distributed data ownership. In this model, the "architectural quantum" shifts from the technical pipeline stage to the business domain itself. Rather than flowing data into a central monolith, domains must host and serve their own datasets, treating data as a product that is discoverable, addressable, trustworthy, and interoperable. This decentralized approach requires a convergence of distributed domain-driven architecture and product thinking, ensuring that data is not siloed in the bowels of operational systems but is managed with Service Level Objectives (SLOs) for quality and integrity.

Data Architecture in the AI Era

AI amplifies both the benefits and challenges of data architecture. AI models require access to diverse, high-quality data across domains—making data mesh's distributed ownership and interoperability standards even more critical. AI training demands historical data at scale, real-time inference requires low-latency data access, and model monitoring needs comprehensive observability. The EA practice must ensure data architecture supports these AI-specific requirements while maintaining governance, security, and business domain ownership. Success is measured not by the sophistication of the data platform, but by the speed and quality of insights delivered to business decision-makers.

A synergy between the Enterprise Architect (EA) and the Data Architect (DA) is essential to executing this distributed vision. While the Enterprise Architect focuses on the broader "Center of Excellence" to foster organic growth and align technical initiatives with fiscal planning and project outcomes, the Data Architect (or data product owner) focuses on the lifecycle, vision, and quality of specific domain datasets. The EA provides the a governance framework to ensure decisions are made rather than just ensuring process compliance, while the DA ensures that data products adhere to global standards for interoperability. Together, they create a "self-serve" infrastructure that allows cross-functional teams to consume and process data autonomously, removing the friction of centralized gatekeepers and allowing the organization to respond rapidly to consumer proliferation and innovation demands.

Communicating Data Architecture to Stakeholders

Data architecture decisions require careful stakeholder translation. When proposing a data mesh transition, the C-Suite needs to understand the business case: faster time-to-insight, reduced dependency on centralized bottlenecks, and improved data quality measured through business KPIs. Business unit leaders need to understand their new accountability for data products and the resources required. Data engineers need technical guidance on implementation patterns. The EA practice bridges these perspectives, ensuring data architecture decisions are understood and supported across all stakeholder groups in terms each group values.

The Data layer operates in concert with the Business, Application and Technology Layers. Together, these layers form the comprehensive BDAT framework that enables measurable business outcomes through effective stakeholder engagement.
